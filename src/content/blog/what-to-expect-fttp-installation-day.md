Your FTTP installation is booked and the date is in the calendar. Now what? A little preparation makes the day go smoothly, avoids a cancelled appointment and, most importantly, gets the equipment installed in a spot that gives you good Wi-Fi for years to come.

This guide walks through the day from start to finish. It applies whether you're connecting a new home, an existing FTTP home that was never activated, or upgrading from FTTN or FTTC.

## Who does what on the day

FTTP installations are booked by your **internet provider** and carried out by **nbn's authorised technicians**. They install nbn's equipment: the fibre lead-in, the utility box outside and the connection box inside. Work beyond that, such as running cabling to another room or setting up mesh Wi-Fi, isn't part of the standard installation, and that's where an independent technician like TerraLink can help, before or after the day.

## Before the day: a preparation checklist

### 1. Decide where you want the connection box

This is the single most important decision. The nbn connection box needs:

- a **power point** within reach (it plugs into a standard outlet);
- a route for the fibre from the outside wall; and
- ideally, a **central location** where your router will give good Wi-Fi coverage.

Common spots are a study, a central hallway cupboard or the living area. Garages and front bedrooms are convenient for the cable route but often leave the back of the house with weak Wi-Fi, especially in double-brick Adelaide homes. If the ideal spot isn't practical for the box, plan to run a data cable from the box to a better router position later.

If you need a new power point, it must be installed by a licensed electrician before the appointment.

### 2. Check the outside access

The technician needs to reach the outside wall where the fibre enters, usually near where your phone line or the existing pit is, and the pit near your boundary. Move cars, bins and anything stacked against the wall. Secure dogs and let the technician know about locked gates.

### 3. Think about the conduit

For underground connections, the fibre is pulled through a conduit from the pit to your house. In older suburbs, that conduit may be decades old, crushed by tree roots or shifted by Adelaide's reactive clay soils. If it's blocked, the installation can't be completed and the private section must be repaired first. If you've had trouble with the phone line before, or the yard has been heavily landscaped, it can be worth having the conduit checked beforehand.

### 4. Make sure an adult is home

Someone aged 18 or over must be home for the whole appointment to give access and agree on equipment locations.

### 5. Tell your alarm or medical alert provider

If you have a monitored alarm or medical alert that uses the phone line, contact its provider before the day to make sure it will work on fibre.

## On the day: step by step

Exact steps vary depending on whether you're a new connection or an upgrade, but a typical installation looks like this. <!-- VERIFY: current nbn FTTP installation steps and whether external and internal work are separate appointments. -->

1. **Arrival and walk-through.** The technician confirms the cable route and where you'd like the equipment.
2. **External work.** Fibre is run from the pit or pole to your house. For underground connections it's pulled through the lead-in conduit; for overhead connections it's attached from the pole to a suitable point on the house.
3. **Utility box.** A small premises connection device is mounted on the outside wall where the fibre arrives.
4. **Wall penetration.** A small hole is drilled through the wall to bring an internal fibre lead inside, and it's sealed afterwards.
5. **Connection box.** The nbn connection box is mounted inside, near the power point, and the fibre is connected.
6. **Testing.** The technician tests the optical signal and confirms the box shows the expected lights.
7. **Handover.** You'll be shown the box and which port to use for your router.

Some upgrades are done in two visits: one for the outside work, which may not need you home, and one for the inside installation. Your provider will tell you what to expect.

## How long does it take?

The installation itself usually takes a few hours. Allow half a day in case of complications. If the conduit is blocked or there's a problem with access, the technician may need to stop and reschedule.

## After the technician leaves

1. **Connect your router** to the correct port on the nbn connection box using an ethernet cable. Your provider will confirm which one; it's usually the first data port.
2. **Wait for activation.** Your provider activates your service, sometimes the same day, sometimes a little later.
3. **Check your router settings.** Many routers need to be set to "ethernet WAN" mode for FTTP. If you came from FTTN, an old modem-router may need reconfiguring or replacing.
4. **Test properly.** Run a speed test on a device connected by cable, not just Wi-Fi, to see what the line delivers.

## Common problems and how to avoid them

- **Blocked conduit.** This is the most common reason installs are cancelled. Fix it beforehand if you suspect a problem.
- **No power point near the preferred location.** Get one installed beforehand.
- **Nobody home.** A missed appointment can mean waiting weeks for another.
- **Box ends up in a poor spot.** Discuss the location with the technician on the day. If it ends up somewhere awkward, cabling can fix it later.
- **Wi-Fi doesn't reach.** A new connection doesn't fix Wi-Fi coverage. Wired mesh access points are usually the best fix in larger homes.
