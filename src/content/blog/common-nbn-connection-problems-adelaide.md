When the internet stops working, the hardest part is often working out **who** can fix it. Is it your router, your provider, nbn's network or the wiring inside your house? Calling the wrong one wastes hours.

This guide walks through the most common nbn problems, how to narrow down the cause, and who to contact for each. It applies to all fixed-line technologies: FTTP, FTTN, FTTC and HFC. If you're not sure which you have, check first using our guide on [how to check your nbn technology](/blog/how-to-check-nbn-technology-adelaide).

## Before anything else: four quick checks

1. **Restart in the right order.** Turn off the router (and the nbn connection box or device, except FTTC; see below). Wait a minute, turn the nbn equipment back on, wait for its lights to settle, then turn the router on.
2. **Check the lights.** On an nbn connection box or device, a red or missing light usually points to a network or line issue rather than your router.
3. **Test on a cable.** Plug a laptop directly into the router with an ethernet cable. If that works well but Wi-Fi doesn't, the problem is Wi-Fi, not the nbn connection.
4. **Check for outages.** Your provider's website or app, and nbn's website, usually list known outages in your area.

> **FTTC homes:** the nbn connection device (NCD) powers equipment in the street pit. Restarting it is fine, but don't leave it switched off or unplugged.

## Problem 1: no internet at all

**Likely causes:** an outage, a fault on nbn's network, equipment failure, or a cable unplugged.

**What to do:**

- Check for outages and restart as above.
- Check every cable: power, the cable from the nbn equipment to the router, and on FTTN and FTTC, the phone cable to the wall socket.
- If the nbn equipment shows a fault light, **contact your provider**. They can run remote tests and, if needed, book an nbn technician. Only your provider can raise faults with nbn.

## Problem 2: slower than your plan

**Likely causes:** Wi-Fi limits, busy-period congestion, your line's maximum speed (FTTN and FTTC), or in-home wiring.

**What to do:**

- Test speed on a **wired** connection, ideally at different times of day.
- If wired speed is fine but Wi-Fi is slow, it's a coverage or router problem (see Problem 4).
- On **FTTN**, speed is limited by distance to the node. Ask your provider for your line's maximum attainable speed; if your plan is faster than your line can do, you're paying for speed you can't get.
- On **FTTN and FTTC**, old extension sockets and internal wiring often reduce speed. That's a job for a technician.
- If it's consistently slow at all times on a wired connection, report it to your provider.

## Problem 3: the connection keeps dropping out

**Likely causes:** line noise on copper, wiring faults, overheating or failing equipment, or on HFC, signal problems.

**What to look for by technology:**

- **FTTN:** drop-outs after rain or in the evening often point to copper problems, either in the street or in the house. Extension sockets, corroded joints and alarm diallers wired into the line are common in-home causes. See [FTTN installation and wiring](/services/fttn-installation-adelaide).
- **FTTC:** check the NCD is plugged into the **main** phone socket, not an extension, and that it isn't on a power board that gets switched off.
- **HFC:** old pay-TV splitters, amplifiers and damaged coaxial cables weaken the signal. See [FTTC and HFC installation](/services/fttc-hfc-installation-adelaide).
- **FTTP:** drop-outs are less common. Check the router and the cable to the connection box, and look for overheating equipment in hot cupboards or garages.

Keep a simple log of when drop-outs happen. Your provider will ask, and it helps a technician find patterns.

## Problem 4: Wi-Fi black spots

**Likely causes:** router location, building materials, or a single router trying to cover a large home.

**What to do:**

- Move the router to a central, open spot, not in a cupboard, on the floor or behind the TV.
- Remember that brick, stone, concrete, foil insulation and mirrors all block Wi-Fi. Many older Adelaide homes have solid masonry walls that Wi-Fi struggles to pass through.
- **Mesh Wi-Fi** helps, but mesh points connected **by cable** work far better than wireless-only mesh.
- If the nbn equipment is in the garage or a front room, have a data cable run to a central router location.

## Problem 5: home phone not working

On the nbn, your home phone usually runs through your router. Check the phone is plugged into the router's phone port (not the wall socket), and that the router is working. If the internet works but the phone doesn't, contact your provider.

## Problem 6: problems after an upgrade or new router

- **New FTTP after FTTN:** your old modem may need to be set to "ethernet WAN" mode, or replaced.
- **New router:** check it's set up with your provider's settings. Security cameras and smart-home devices may need reconnecting to the new Wi-Fi.

## Who to call: a quick guide

| Symptom | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Red or missing light on nbn equipment | Your internet provider |
| Area-wide outage | Your provider (check their status page) |
| Slow on Wi-Fi, fine on cable | Your router or a technician for Wi-Fi coverage |
| Slow or dropping on cable, FTTN or FTTC | Provider first; if they say the fault is "in-premises", a technician |
| Old extension sockets, alarm wiring, splitters | A technician |
| nbn box or router in a bad location | A technician for cabling |
| Blocked conduit stopping an installation | A technician |

## How to report a fault so it gets fixed faster

When you call your provider, have ready: your service details, your nbn technology, what the lights on the nbn equipment show, when the problem started, whether it's constant or intermittent, and the results of a wired speed test. Say what you've already tried. This can skip several rounds of "have you restarted the modem?".
